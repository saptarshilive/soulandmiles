How a journey gets made

There is no catalogue to choose from. Every path starts with a conversation and ends up somewhere only you would have asked for.

1

Tell us the shape of it

A week in the hills with your parents. Ten quiet days somewhere green. A river, a temple, and no schedule at all. Feelings are enough to start.

2

We draft the path

You get it in writing — routes, stays, drive times, where you'll eat, how hard the walking gets. Then you change whatever doesn't sit right.

3

We walk it with you

Local hosts and guides we know by name, one coordinator reachable through the trip, and room left in the day for what you didn't plan.

What we hold to

Slow

Fewer places, longer stays. Three nights somewhere ordinary teaches you more than eight sights in four days.

Offbeat

Valleys, trails and towns that never made the circuit. If a place is crowded, we go a valley further or a season earlier.

Hosted

Homestays and family-run places wherever they exist, so the money stays in the village and someone eats with you.

Small

Your own group, or a handful of people at most. Never a coach, never a flag, never a stranger's itinerary.

Paths we've already walked

These were built for particular people, with particular reasons. Yours won't look like any of them — but they show the range we work across.

Milan Mistry

Milan has spent thirty years on high-altitude trails — Everest Base Camp, Yankar Pass at 15,000 ft, the Valley of Flowers at 14,400, Hamta Pass, Brahmatal, Roopkund — and rather longer sitting in kitchens in small towns, working out who to trust with other people's holidays.

She plans every Curated Paths journey herself and travels most of them. If you write in, she is the one who replies.

The best trips aren't the ones we designed. They're the ones someone described to us badly, and we understood anyway.

Not age — spirit decides which way the journey goes.

That was the headline when a Gujarati daily profiled Milan at 64: twenty-five hard treks in thirty-two years, starting with Pachmarhi in 1994 on a Youth Hostels trip, and a job left behind somewhere along the way.

The paper also carried the part she doesn't bring up herself. Covid took most of her lung capacity. At sixty she bought herself a bicycle so that altitude would stay possible, and she has been riding thirty kilometres, three mornings a week, ever since.
